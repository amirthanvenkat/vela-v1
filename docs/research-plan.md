# VELA: usability session kit

*Everything needed to run five 30-minute sessions with first-salary users: who to recruit, what to
say, six tasks, a notes template and the pass marks. No sessions have been run yet. Findings go in
`docs/research-findings.md` once they have.*

---

## What the sessions are for

VELA's roadmap rests on estimates. The [prioritisation log](prioritisation.md) rates the garden and
the goal suggestions at 50% confidence and calls them hunches. These sessions replace the hunches
with observations.

Four questions to answer:

1. Do people read the garden as "my habit" or as "my returns"?
2. Do they believe, and can they find, "take it out whenever you want"?
3. Does the shares and bonds mix make sense in the words used?
4. Is the "keep it in your Savings Pot" tip read as helpful or as a brush-off?

A fifth check rides along: does VELA install and open offline on a real phone?

---

## Who to recruit

Five people. Each should match all of these:

- In their first or second year of full-time work.
- Has money left at the end of most months, however little.
- Has never invested, or has tried once and stopped.
- Has a smartphone they are willing to use in the session.

Avoid people who work in finance or product design, and aim for at least three who are not close
friends. Friends are kinder than strangers, and kindness hides problems.

**Screener, to send before booking:**

1. How long have you been in full-time work?
2. Have you ever put money into shares, funds or a robo-adviser? (Never / once or twice / regularly)
3. What kind of phone do you use?

Take "never" or "once or twice". Try for a mix of iPhone and Android.

## Before each session

- [ ] Send the participant the live link: `https://amirthanvenkat.github.io/vela-v1/`
- [ ] Have the garden link ready to send during task 2:
      `https://amirthanvenkat.github.io/vela-v1/#/invest?demo&flowers=1&ages=0,1,2`
- [ ] Ask them not to open either link before the session.
- [ ] Have a copy of the notes template open, named P1 to P5. No real names in the notes.
- [ ] If the session is remote, ask them to join from a laptop and hold the phone up, or to share
      their phone screen.

The garden link opens a seeded demo of the Invest screen: SGD 200.00 in a garden of four flowers
(one in bloom, plus a seedling, a sprout and a bud), next to the starting seed. It reads "4 flowers
so far" and "Next bloom in about 1 day". Demo links do not read or overwrite saved progress, so it
is safe to open mid-session.

The link only works when it loads as a fresh page. Tapping it in a message opens a new tab, which
is fine. Pasting it over the address in the tab where VELA is already open does not load the demo.
If that happens, reload the page.

## What to say at the start

Read this out, or say it in your own words:

> Thanks for doing this. I'm testing a prototype of a money app called VELA. I'm testing the app,
> not you, so there are no wrong answers. If something is confusing, that's the app's fault and
> exactly what I need to hear.
>
> Nothing in it is real. The money is pretend and nothing connects to your bank. Please don't type
> any real account details.
>
> As you go, say what you're thinking out loud: what you expect, what surprises you, what you'd tap
> next. I may stay quiet when you ask me something. That's so I can see what you'd do on your own.
>
> I'll take written notes. I won't use your name, and the notes will only be used to improve this
> prototype and in a short write-up of what I learned. You can stop at any time. Is that all right?

Wait for a clear yes. Only record audio or the screen if you ask separately and they agree.

## Rules for the facilitator

- Give the goal, never the button. Say "put some money aside", not "tap Save".
- When they ask "what does this do?", answer "what would you expect it to do?".
- Count to five before helping. If you do help, mark the task as **hinted**.
- Do not explain the garden, the mix or any term until the wrap-up.
- Write down what they do first, then what they say.

---

## The six tasks

About 20 minutes in total. The timings are a guide.

### Task 1: get started and invest (5 min)

**Say:** "A friend sent you this link. Open it and have a look around. When you're ready, put a
small amount of money into investing."

**Watch for:**
- Which character they pick on the first question, and whether they hesitate.
- Whether they find **Plant a flower** without help.
- What they do with the amount, and whether they read the line about taking money out.
- Their reaction to the "first flower planted" moment.

**Then ask:** "What just happened to your money?"

**Pass:** onboarding and a first investment completed with no hint.

### Task 2: explain the garden (3 min)

**Say:** "I'm sending you a second link. It shows a garden a few days further on." Send the garden
link.

**Ask, in this order:**
1. "What is this screen telling you?"
2. "Why are some flowers bigger than others?"
3. "If the market had a bad week, what would happen to this garden?"
4. "Is there anything you'd want to know here that you can't see?"

**Watch for:** the word they reach for. "Growing", "time" and "days" suggest habit. "Profit",
"returns" and "making money" suggest they read it as performance.

**Pass:** they say the flowers grow with time or with planting, not with profit. Note separately
whether they ask how much their money is worth.

When done, ask them to close this tab and go back to the first one. Their own progress is still
there.

### Task 3: save for something (4 min)

**Say:** "You want to save for something that matters to you, with a rough date in mind. Set that
up, and put some money towards it."

**Watch for:**
- Whether they find **Set a Goal**, and whether they add the "by when" month.
- Whether they use the preset amounts or type their own.

**Then say:** "Now go to invest some more." Let them reach the amount screen.

**Ask:** "What is this tip telling you? Would you follow it?"

The tip depends on their date. A goal three or more years away suggests a monthly amount. A nearer
goal suggests keeping the money in the Savings Pot. Note which one they saw. If all of the first
three participants see the same one, ask the last two to pick a date that shows the other.

**Pass:** goal set and money added with no hint. Record word for word how they describe the tip,
and whether "a guide, not advice" registers.

### Task 4: get money back (3 min)

**Say:** "Your laptop has just died and you need about 60 dollars back from what you invested. Get
it."

**Watch for:**
- Where they look first: Home, Invest or You.
- Whether they notice which flowers will go before confirming.
- Whether they read the line about 1 to 3 working days.

**Then ask:** "How long would you expect this to take in real life? Would anything be taken off?"

**Pass:** withdrawal completed with no hint.

### Task 5: what your money is in (3 min)

**Say:** "Find out what your invested money is actually in."

**Ask:** "In your own words, what are shares? What are bonds? What does this mix mean for you?"

**Then say:** "Say you wanted to be a bit bolder, or a bit safer. Change it. You can back out
before confirming if you like."

**Watch for:** whether they read the "your mix moves from" line, and how they describe what will
change.

**Pass:** they can say what the mix means for them, roughly right, without reading it off the
screen.

### Task 6: install and go offline (2 min)

**Say:** "Add VELA to your home screen, the way you would with an app you wanted to keep."

If they do not know how, show them and mark it as **hinted**. On iPhone it is Share, then **Add to
Home Screen**. On Android it is the browser menu, then **Install app**.

**Then say:** "Turn on aeroplane mode and open it from the icon."

**Watch for:** whether it opens full screen, whether their name, balance and garden are still
there, and anything that looks broken.

**Pass:** opens offline from the icon with their progress intact. Note the phone model and browser.

---

## Wrap-up questions (5 min)

1. "How would you describe this app to a friend?"
2. "What was the most confusing moment?"
3. "Was there a point where you trusted it less? More?"
4. "What would you expect it to do that it didn't?"
5. "Would you put real money in something like this? What would have to be true first?"

Thank them. Now you can answer any questions they had along the way.

## Pass marks

Set before the sessions so the results cannot bend them.

| Task | What counts | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Get started and invest | Done with no hint | 4 of 5 |
| 2. Explain the garden | Says time or habit, not profit | 4 of 5 |
| 3. Save for something | Goal set and money added with no hint | 4 of 5 |
| 4. Get money back | Done with no hint | 4 of 5 |
| 5. What your money is in | Explains the mix in their own words | 3 of 5 |
| 6. Install and go offline | Opens offline with progress intact | 5 of 5 devices |

Five people is a small group. Report results as counts ("4 of 5"), never as percentages. If a task
splits 3 to 2, run two more sessions on that task before deciding anything.

---

## After the sessions

1. Within a day of each session, tidy the notes while the detail is fresh.
2. After the fifth, fill in the table above with the real counts.
3. Group the observations across participants. An issue seen in three or more sessions is a
   finding. One seen once is a note.
4. Write `docs/research-findings.md`: what was tested, the counts, the findings in order of how
   many people hit them, and one quote for each.
5. Re-score the open items in the prioritisation log, changing Confidence where the sessions give
   grounds to, and choose the next build item from the result.
